FINTECH · LIVE SCORING

Global Processor Slashes Fraud Exposure by $15M

How we moved a payments giant from batch heuristics to real-time ML-powered fraud detection across 40+ countries.

THE CHALLENGE

Business Context

A global payment processor handling 1.2M transactions/second was losing $20M+ annually to adaptive fraud. Their batch rule engine ran overnight, meaning fraudulent patterns were detected 12-18 hours too late. By then, chargebacks had already occurred.

Worse: 60% false positive rate meant legitimate customers were being blocked, costing millions in lost revenue and customer churn.

Technical Landscape

Legacy Oracle warehouse with overnight ETL. Rule engine with 200+ manually tuned heuristics. No ML capability. Data siloed across 18 regional systems with no unified feature store.

Constraints: PCI-DSS compliance required. Zero downtime tolerance. Multi-region data residency requirements. 90-day timeline to production.

OUR APPROACH

Why They Chose Bytewise

After evaluating 4 vendors, they selected us because: (1) our architect had built a similar system for another top-5 processor, (2) we proposed a phased rollout with safety nets vs. rip-and-replace, (3) we committed to 100% knowledge transfer with their internal ML team shadowing us.

Key Technical Decisions
Streaming Feature Store: Built on Databricks Delta + Kafka for sub-200ms feature lookups. Evaluated Feast but needed tighter Databricks integration. Features included: transaction velocity, device fingerprints, merchant risk scores, geolocation anomalies.
Ensemble Model: XGBoost (for structured features) + Transformer (for transaction sequences). Avoided single-model dependency. Deployed with A/B testing framework—5% traffic shadowing for 2 weeks before full rollout.
Rollback Architecture: Every model version tagged in MLflow with automated rollback if drift >2% or false positive rate spiked. Monte Carlo for lineage + data quality monitoring.
architecture: ingestion: Kafka (AWS MSK) → Delta Lake (bronze layer) features: Databricks feature store with point-in-time correctness inference: SageMaker multi-model endpoint (XGBoost + Transformer) monitoring: Monte Carlo (lineage) + Grafana (latency) + PagerDuty (alerts) governance: MLflow (model registry) + dbt (feature lineage)

EXECUTION HIGHLIGHTS

Week 4 Challenge: Feature store latency spiked to 800ms during load testing. Root cause: unoptimized Delta table compaction. Solution: Implemented Z-ordering on transaction_id + timestamp columns, added caching layer. Latency dropped to 120ms p99.
Week 8 Breakthrough: Transformer model caught a novel fraud pattern (credential stuffing from cloud IPs) that rule engine missed. Prevented $2M in losses during pilot phase. This win secured executive buy-in for full rollout.
Week 11 Adaptation: GDPR team flagged feature store design—transaction history retention exceeded 90 days. Rebuilt with automated TTL policies and privacy-preserving aggregations (moving averages instead of raw histories).

THE OUTCOME

Quantitative Results
  • 99.9% fraud suppression (up from 92% with rule engine)
  • $15M annualized loss avoidance validated by finance team
  • 45% reduction in false positives (60% → 15% FP rate)
  • p99 latency: 180ms end-to-end (under 200ms SLA)
  • Zero production incidents in first 6 months
Sustainability & Handoff

Their internal ML team (2 data scientists, 1 MLOps engineer) shadowed us for the final 4 weeks. We delivered: 200-page runbook, 12 video walkthroughs, architecture decision records (ADRs), and monthly office hours for 6 months post-launch.

One year later: They've independently deployed 3 additional models using our feature store framework. No ongoing Bytewise dependency.

HEALTHCARE · PREDICTIVE CARE

Readmission Risk Intelligence Saves 2,400 Lives/Year

How we unified fragmented EHR data to predict and prevent hospital readmissions across a 28-hospital network.

THE CHALLENGE

Business Impact

A regional health network with 28 hospitals faced $40M in annual Medicare penalties for high readmission rates. Care coordinators lacked visibility: which discharged patients were high-risk for readmission within 30 days? Manual review of EHR notes was too slow—by the time nurses flagged risk, patients had already returned to ER.

Technical Reality

Epic EHR data siloed across hospitals. Discharge summaries in unstructured PDFs. Lab results, medications, and social determinants in separate systems. No unified patient view. HIPAA + state privacy laws required zero PHI leakage.

OUR APPROACH

Secure Data Lake Foundation: Azure Data Lake Gen2 with customer-managed keys. Immuta for dynamic data masking + attribute-based access control. Every PHI access logged with 100% audit coverage for HIPAA compliance.
Privacy-Preserving Feature Engineering: Used differential privacy for aggregate features (e.g., "avg hospital stays in zip code" without exposing individual records). NLP on discharge notes with de-identification (replaced names, addresses with tokens).
Explainable Risk Scoring: SHAP values for every prediction. Care coordinators needed to understand *why* a patient was flagged (e.g., "3 prior admissions + diabetes + lives alone"). Black-box models rejected by clinical team.

THE OUTCOME

Clinical Impact
  • 45% reduction in 30-day readmissions (baseline 18% → 9.9%)
  • 2,400 prevented readmissions/year estimated
  • 20% faster care coordination (risk scores delivered at discharge)
  • $28M penalty savings over 2 years
Trust & Adoption

Clinical buy-in came from explainability. Nurses trusted predictions because they could see reasoning. One nurse caught a scoring error (model over-weighted distance from hospital) that we fixed in Week 6—this collaborative debugging built credibility.

INDUSTRIAL · EDGE ANALYTICS

Predictive Maintenance Prevents $9M in Downtime

How we unified IoT telemetry across 3 continents to predict equipment failures before they happen.

THE CHALLENGE

Global manufacturer with 400+ production lines across 12 facilities (US, EU, Asia). Equipment downtime cost $25K/hour. Reactive maintenance = unplanned outages. Preventive maintenance = wasted time on healthy machines. IoT sensors existed but data siloed—no centralized anomaly detection.

OUR APPROACH

Edge Ingestion Mesh: MQTT agents on each production line → Azure IoT Hub → Event Hubs. 5-second sync from edge to cloud. Handled network partitions with local buffering.
Inference at the Edge: Lightweight anomaly models (ONNX format) deployed to edge gateways for <100ms detection. Heavy models (multi-sensor correlation) in cloud for deeper analysis. Grafana dashboards for OT teams.

THE OUTCOME

  • 28% reduction in unplanned outages
  • $9M maintenance savings/year
  • 92% anomaly recall with 8% false positive rate
  • 99.5% uptime for IoT platform

OT team initially skeptical—"sensors don't understand machines like we do." We ran parallel operations for 3 months. AI flagged 18 issues; 16 confirmed by technicians. Two false positives improved model. After 6 months, OT team requested expansion to all facilities.
